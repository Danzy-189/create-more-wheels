# tractor_tire

Agricultural tractor tyre: a very tall black carcass on a dished red steel rim, with
eleven deep chevron lugs. The largest wheel in the lineup, registered against Offroad's
`large_tire` tier at 1.25.

## The tread

A lugged tread is the one shape that can be a perfectly closed solid and still look full
of holes, because along the axle you see straight through any gap between two lugs. A
purely radial lug covers only its own narrow slice of angle, so a radial-lug tread is
either a sieve or has its lugs touching - which is the compromise `ural_tire` had to
make.

This tyre slants them instead. Eleven chevrons, each a pair of arms climbing from
opposite shoulders and converging on the same angle at the crown, each arm a five step
staircase sweeping 30 degrees of angle against a 32.7 degree lug pitch. Because the
sweep exceeds the pitch, consecutive lugs overlap in the axial projection and the tread
closes completely, while the surface still shows 16.9 degrees of clear groove between
neighbouring chevrons.

Closure is verified numerically at build time - 33 radii by 2160 angles, every direction
tested against every lug - rather than from a formula. An approximate formula is exactly
what once let a 0.075 px ring of daylight through the ural tyre.

Relief is 1.16 px, 13.7% of the wheel radius and roughly twice what the ural carries.
The lugs stay 0.04 blocks inside the widest part of the sidewall at both shoulders, so
the tread never reads as bursting out of the tyre.

## Three tones of rubber

The first version of this model used twenty shallow lugs of four segments and rendered
as blocky rubble rather than as a tread: too many pieces, too little relief, and every
rubber surface the same near-black, so the pattern existed only in the flat-shaded
normals and disappeared completely head-on and in silhouette.

The fix was fewer and bigger lugs, more relief, and splitting the rubber into three
tones - dark carcass, darker groove floor, lighter worn crown. Fitting that fifth tile
is the only reason this is the first wheel here on a 64x64 atlas rather than 32x32.

## Geometry

Generated by `build_tractor.py`, which takes the name and target radius as arguments and
emits this wheel and `small-tractor_tire` from one source. The design space is 16 px
with the axle on +Z; the writer scales by 2.1812, then rotates -90 degrees about X so
the axle ends up on +Y and the mesh is centred on the block.

- 1642 faces, 6456 vertices, quads only
- mesh radius 1.156 blocks against a registered radius of 1.25, a 7.5% margin
- bounds 2.312 x 0.8561 x 2.3091 blocks, width over diameter 0.37
- rim reaches about half the outer radius, which is what makes the tyre read as
  agricultural

The rim is a plain bead barrel with a lip at each end, then a centre disc stepping back
toward the axle through three concentric rings to a bolt circle and a small cap. The
reverse carries a flat web, a protruding boss and six bolt heads, so the back is not a
featureless black disc.

## Texture

One 64x64 atlas, `tire_0.png`, using five 16x16 tiles: carcass rubber, rim red, groove
shadow, dark rim metal and worn tread crown. The red is sampled off the reference photo,
whose rim medians RGB (114, 50, 45), lifted slightly so it still reads as red after
shading and given scuffs and grime so it does not look like flat plastic. `item.json`
sets `flip_v: true`, so the OBJ stores v bottom-up.

## Checks

The generator refuses to write a face with more than four corners. The NeoForge OBJ
loader assembles quads and silently drops anything wider, which shows up in game as a
hole rather than as an error. It also checks tread closure, lug overhang, UV containment,
degenerate faces and coplanar caps that would z-fight. A render pass then confirms zero
see-through pixels from six camera angles, front and back, and that every primitive is
closed and consistently wound.
